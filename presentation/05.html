First, verify the complete path from service signals to team response.
Check production and CAP coverage separately.

Proposed improvements

  • Make useful dashboards, logs and traces available
    for each agreed product and environment.
  • Assess alerts by user impact and urgency.
    Record and manage service disruptions as incidents.
  • Assign response owners, backups and user communication
    with agreed support hours and targets.
  • Verify recovery, investigate recurring issues
    and measure whether the process improves.

Build on the tools we have. Fix gaps before adding more tools.

Incident records and communication

AreaWhat we agree
Incident recordRecord the service, environment, version and start time.
Add impact, priority, owner and actions taken.
Response targetsAgree support hours and targets for acknowledgement,
escalation and service restoration for each priority.
CommunicationName who updates affected users, where and how often.
For major incidents, appoint a lead to coordinate the response.
ClosureConfirm recovery. Record the outcome and any workaround.
Communicate closure and assign remaining follow-up work.

Use one incident record for related alerts. Keep response instructions linked to it.

Proving that alerts work

TrialWhat must happen
Controlled failureThe real recipient receives and acknowledges the alert.
The team records the incident, priority and owner.
Missing signalsThe agreed no-data alert appears.
Missing data must not look like a healthy service.
No response or failed deliveryThe backup or escalation path reaches a responsible person.
Check the agreed response timing and user updates.
RecoveryFresh signals and the user journey confirm recovery.
The team records closure and groups duplicate alerts.

Repeat the relevant checks after changing rules, recipients or ownership.

Environment and data rules

AreaProposed rule
QA and maintenanceKeep test alerts separate from customer incidents.
Limit silences to a target and expiry time, then test their expiry.
Production, demos and pilotsAgree service monitoring, support coverage and recipients.
Keep QA silences separate from active customer environments.
Data and costRedact sensitive content. Restrict access and agree retention.
Set collection limits and review monitoring cost.

Remove alert targets when a QA instance ends. Keep alerts for unexpected failures.

Learning from incidents

WorkHow we handle it
Service recoveryRestore service promptly with a safe fix or workaround.
Confirm recovery and complete the incident record.
Problem investigationOpen a linked problem record for recurring or serious issues.
Investigate causes and record known errors and workarounds.
PreventionAssign an owner to each corrective action.
Review and test changes through the agreed change process.
Track longer-term investigation separately from service recovery.
Update response guides and alert rules with what we learn.

How we measure improvement

MeasureWhat we track
Acknowledgement timeTime from notification to a responder acknowledging the alert.
Service recovery timeTime from incident start to confirmed service recovery.
Repeat incidentsHow often the same issue returns after recovery or a fix.
Alert qualityAlerts that need action, duplicate noise and incidents
that monitoring missed.
Capture a baseline and agree targets with service owners.
Review results regularly and assign owners to improvements.

Decisions for the team

  1. Which service, environment and user journey should we cover first?
  2. What priorities, support hours and response targets
    should service owners agree?
  3. Who responds, backs up and coordinates major incidents,
    and who updates affected users?
  4. Where will we track incidents, recurring problems
    and improvement actions, with the right data access?

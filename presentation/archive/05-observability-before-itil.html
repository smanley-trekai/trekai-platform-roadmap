Proposed improvements

  • Make useful dashboards, logs and traces available
    for each agreed product and environment.
  • Alert on failures that affect users and require action.
  • Give each alert a responsible team, a backup
    and clear response instructions.
  • Test alert delivery and confirm the service recovers.

Build on the tools we have. Fix gaps before adding more tools.

InformationWhat it helps us understand

Identify the product, environment, service and deployed version.
QA also records the test instance and release manifest.

Proving that alerts work

TrialWhat must happen
Controlled failureThe expected alert reaches the real recipient.
A responder acknowledges it and takes ownership.
Missing signalsThe agreed no-data alert appears.
Missing data must not look like a healthy service.
Failed delivery or no responseThe backup or escalation path reaches a responsible person.
RecoveryFresh signals and the user journey confirm recovery.
Duplicate alerts stop or group as intended.

Repeat the relevant checks after changing rules, recipients or ownership.

Environment and data rules

AreaProposed rule
QA and maintenanceKeep test alerts separate from customer incidents.
Limit silences to a target and expiry time, then test their expiry.
Production, demos and pilotsAgree service monitoring, support coverage and recipients.
Keep QA silences separate from active customer environments.
Data and costRedact sensitive content. Restrict access and agree retention.
Set collection limits and review monitoring cost.

Remove alert targets when a QA instance ends. Keep alerts for unexpected failures.

Decisions for the team

  1. Which service, environment and user journey should we cover first?
  2. Which failures require immediate action,
    and which can wait for normal working hours?
  3. Who receives alerts, who is the backup,
    and when should an unanswered alert escalate?
  4. What data access, retention and collection limits should we use?
